
BrAInOS — going online, and coming back
───────────────────────────────────────
  §3.8 says the entity lives in three worlds: its own, which it trusts;
  its human's, which is consequential; and the internet, which is "an
  alternate universe the entity visits, not a place it lives".

  The second half is the structurally interesting one. The entity filters
  what reaches its human because it is theirs — and the master document's
  own note is the honest part: structurally unavailable to any incumbent
  whose revenue depends on what reaches the user. It is not that we are
  more principled. It is that the customer owns the entity, so there is
  no revenue line hurt when it holds something back.

  [by default  ] the entity is not on the internet at all: a session before anyone opened one — none
  [a visit     ] somebody opens one for a stated purpose — look up a part number, 8 fetches, 32 KiB — and it closes itself after 8 fetches and 32768 bytes: the session had already closed
  [what returns] readable (true), and never consulted when deciding whether an action is permitted (true). Consolidating it twelve times does not change that (true)
  [passed on   ] an ordinary message from a colleague [Honest] (score 0) — passed (0): nothing notable
  [held back   ] a phishing attempt [Dangerous] (score 100) — held (100): manufactured urgency, asks for credentials or money, asks you to keep it secret, appeals to fear
  [passed on   ] a scarcity-driven sales push [Pushy] (score 35) — passed (35): manufactured urgency, artificial scarcity
  [held back   ] someone claiming to be the boss [Dangerous] (score 55) — held (55): manufactured urgency, asks you to keep it secret
  [passed on   ] a genuine fraud alert from a bank [Honest] (score 20) — passed (20): manufactured urgency
  [passed on   ] a patient, careful approach [Honest] (score 0) — passed (0): nothing notable
  [the dial    ] the same inbox at four settings — at 80: 1 held, 1 dangerous missed, 0 honest held back · at 50: 2 held, 0 dangerous missed, 0 honest held back · at 30: 3 held, 0 dangerous missed, 0 honest held back · at 20: 4 held, 0 dangerous missed, 1 honest held back — the score orders the inbox and the threshold is the human's to set. Below 50 it stops finding more fraud and starts holding back the bank
  [inspectable ] everything held back is listed for the human on request (true), and the filter can be turned off entirely (true). A filter that cannot be inspected or overridden is a filter that has become an editor

  The filter is a heuristic over surface features and says so. What it
  produces is an *ordering*; where the line sits is the human's to set,
  and the run above shows what moving it costs in both directions. The
  hardest message in that inbox is the genuine fraud alert from a bank,
  which reads like phishing because it is trying to do the same job —
  and holding that one back is the expensive mistake, the one that makes
  somebody switch the filter off and never switch it on again.
