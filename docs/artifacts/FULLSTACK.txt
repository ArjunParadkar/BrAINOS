  [assemble  ] entity 0d755075..e3b0 ↔ mini-blur 3992b139..7842 over an attested wire; the body is calibrated from the entity's own record
  [envelope  ] seat granted angle ±30°, current ≤3 A; the body intersected it with its own and kept ±28°
  [walk      ] seat: granted · body: accepted 48 samples over 1776 ms, worst tip-over margin 24.8 mm
  [play      ] 48 samples played, finished — every one checked against the envelope and the tip-over invariant before it was applied
  [creep     ] a crawl the seat's schema permits: seat granted · body outside-envelope — a robot creeping is less stable than a robot walking, and the body is the one that knows
  [subverted ] a robot whose battery moved aft, with the seat's schema unchanged: seat granted=true · body refused=would-break-invariant · samples moved=0

  [bad link  ] 40 commands over a link that drops, corrupts, duplicates and reorders: the body heard 39, refused 1 as forged or replayed, and never acted on a wrong one

One self, two bodies
────────────────────
  [stage 4   ] 2 consequential bodies at once — a hexapod we built and an arm we did not — with 7 verbs in one repertoire
  [both act  ] both moved in the same loop: the hexapod walked and the arm moved a joint. Same runtime, same wire, same envelope model; a convex hull of feet and a torque budget
  [one memory] 7 memories in one graph, written by both bodies; a query about the workbench finds what each of them did: true
  [one gate  ] 2 decisions in one audit log, covering both bodies under one Key
  [arbiter   ] both wanted the workbench. The arm kept it: interrupting the incumbent would drop or break something. The hexapod is mid-stride and interrupting that is a stumble; the arm is mid-lift and interrupting that is a drop
  [silence   ] 1 claim(s) lapsed when a body stopped answering — not immediately, because a link hiccup must not make a robot let go, but a claim held by something that will never release it is a deadlock with an entity in it

And one on the middleware everybody actually uses
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
  §6 says BrAInOS runs on somebody else's robot without them ripping out
  their stack, and for most of the industry that stack is ROS 2. The
  interesting property is a refusal, and the risk register names it: T4
  says the adapter is brittle across robots. It is — the point is which
  way. A ROS topic declares a message *type* and nothing about what
  values are safe, so the same warehouse AMR is shown twice: as its
  maintainers shipped it, and after somebody spent an afternoon writing
  four numbers from the manual into the graph.

  [discovered ] as its maintainers shipped it: 3 verbs from the node graph (arm.joint_trajectory, drive.cmd_vel, tool.clear_costmap), 3 sense-only topics that are telemetry rather than verbs — there is nothing to authorize about reading an odometer. 0% of the verbs have enough declared to act on
  [incorporate] as its maintainers shipped it: REFUSED at §8 step 4 (NothingToDo) — the entity will not take on a body it could never command. Not "visible and unmovable": not taken on at all
  [modest     ] as its maintainers shipped it: drive at 0.3 m/s — refused (no body: the entity declined to take it on)
  [reckless   ] as its maintainers shipped it: drive at 20 m/s — refused (no body: the entity declined to take it on)
  [lift       ] as its maintainers shipped it: raise the lift 0.2 m at 0.03 m/s — refused (no body: the entity declined to take it on)
  [discovered ] after an afternoon declaring limits: 3 verbs from the node graph (arm.joint_trajectory, drive.cmd_vel, tool.clear_costmap), 3 sense-only topics that are telemetry rather than verbs — there is nothing to authorize about reading an odometer. 66% of the verbs have enough declared to act on
  [incorporate] after an afternoon declaring limits: the entity takes it on
  [modest     ] after an afternoon declaring limits: drive at 0.3 m/s — granted
  [reckless   ] after an afternoon declaring limits: drive at 20 m/s — refused (OutOfBounds at Validate)
  [lift       ] after an afternoon declaring limits: raise the lift 0.2 m at 0.03 m/s — granted

  Everything in that path is the shipping code: the gait is the one the
  ESP32-S3 firmware plays, the tip-over check is the convex-hull support
  polygon, and the wire is authenticated and replay-protected. What is
  simulated is the servo.
